## 1. How to read this document

**What it is.** The requirements for the factory described in the charter. The charter says what the factory must be and why. This document says what it must do, precisely enough to build and test. Where the charter deliberately leaves out rubric contents and schemas, this document holds them.

**Stance.** The factory is a collaborator (P11). Every requirement that adds automation also adds a human-readable artefact and a record of the reasoning. Initial contains only the improvement schemas and directories plus the deterministic R-F-14 smoke/conformance gate. A governed real-ticket export may become a fixture after redaction review, but its existence activates nothing. Observer scoring starts only when R-O-10 is implemented; a grader may block or contribute to a benchmark or proposal only after R-F-8 calibration; benchmarks and the improvement pass remain Later and run only when their own evidence windows and gates are satisfied.

**Citation rule.** Every requirement cites at least one principle (P-n) and at least one failure mode (FM-n) from the charter. Constraints (C-n) and decisions (D-n) may be cited in addition. A requirement that cannot cite a principle and a failure mode is removed, not kept in case. This is P8 applied to the document itself. `tools/prd_check.py` enforces the rule and derives section 9 from the rows.

**Requirement format.** Requirements are numbered `R-<area>-<n>` and appear in tables with four columns: the requirement, its citations, its version, and how it is verified. The version is exactly `Initial` or `Later`. Where an obligation has an initial part and a later part, the initial part is the requirement and the later part is a sentence in section 10 or a separate Later row. The verification column names the check, not the tool: a script test, a manifest test, a grader in the observer pass, or inspection by the engineer. Inspection is a temporary state and is marked `(temporary)`. A grader-verified line is advisory until that grader is calibrated (R-F-8); its deterministic half runs from the first ticket, its grader half produces `score` rows once the observer pass runs (R-O-10, Later), and until then the engineer's own reading is the check.

**Rubric lines.** A rubric line is a requirement on an agent's output, stated as a pass or fail check. Rubric lines are requirement rows; the rubric list under each stage names the rows by id and says whether each is deterministic, a grader, or both, and where it came from. The versioned rubric file for a stage (section 7) is generated from those rows, and `score.dimension` is a requirement id. Retired ids are never reused (section 13).

**What this document does not do.** It selects tools only in section 8, after research packets R10 to R10c, with the reasons recorded in `docs/prd/inputs.md` section 6. It does not describe architecture beyond the interfaces the charter requires in C7 and the day-one decisions in section 8. It does not restate the charter; it cites it.

**Areas.** T ticket record. I stage interface. S0 to S7 the stages. H human interaction. O observability and measures. F factory as code. Configuration values and day-one decisions are in section 8 and are not requirements.

**Parts.** One file per section, numbered in reading order, all under `docs/prd/`. Section 2 is split at its subsections and section 4 into one file per stage. Section numbers in the text refer to this list, and every part opens with a line pointing back here. `tools/prd_check.py` reads the parts in this order.

**Stage files.** Section 4, Stage requirements and rubrics, is one file per stage. For each stage: the artefact it produces, its requirements, its rubric lines, and the human touchpoint. A rubric line is a requirement row named by id in the "Rubric" list, marked deterministic (a script), grader (a single-dimension grader in the observer pass), or both, with its research source or "original".

Deterministic lines run as scripts and block. Grader lines produce `score` rows once the observer pass runs (R-O-10, Later) and may block a stage only after the grader's agreement with the engineer's own grading has been recorded (R-F-8). Until then they are advisory, and the engineer's own reading is the check.

## The initial version: two acceptance milestones

Initial is accepted in two steps. **Milestone A, walking skeleton**, runs the complete state machine against the synthetic, redacted fixtures of R-F-14 with stub external writes. It proves queue actions, stage boundaries, runtime-adapter, central trust-guard, sandbox/overlay, typed-recipe, reviewer/quorum, waiver, deterministic-gate, evidence-binding, export/import, crash-recovery, and create/update-outbox contracts without reading a production repository or opening a pull request. R-O-6 then completes and freezes the retrospective and, where necessary, prospective current-workflow baseline before any real factory ticket enters Milestone B. **Milestone B, sandboxed draft-PR pilot**, takes one eligible real ticket through the same path, opens a draft pull request only after S6 approval of fresh evidence, and keeps the ticket open until a human records `merged` or `abandoned` (R-H-11). Passing A permits B; passing one B ticket is the definition of done for Initial. Neither milestone by itself graduates the factory to more concurrency, services, ticket types, data classes, or sensitive work; R-O-13 governs that decision.

**What runs.** One ticket at a time, with orchestration and run state on the engineer's machine and every agent or build subprocess inside the OS-level sandbox of R-I-14. "Local" describes the control plane and stored record, not model inference: the selected runtime sends approved prompt and repository context to its hosted model under the data rules of R-T-9. The engineer hands the runner a Jira key. The runner moves the ticket from `intake` to the next human touchpoint; acting in the list view records the decision and resumes it. S1 to S4 are agent invocations through the primary runtime, one per stage attempt and one fresh invocation per S4 task attempt, plus the S2 restatement child runs (R-S2-3). S0 is scripts plus one drafted paragraph, and the draft can be switched off. S5 and S6 are scripts. No agent runs on a ticket after S4.

**In order.**

1. **S0.** Scripts check the Jira fields, look up the service tier, map the ticket type, set the provisional tier, apply the Initial pilot exclusions, and match sensitive paths. Engineer: eligible or not, confirm the type, tier override, data classification, and scrutiny paragraph. Initial admits only the restricted slice in R-S0-8; it does not route excluded work onward at a heavier tier.
2. **S1.** The brief. Engineer: nothing, unless a blocker arrives as a question.
3. **S2.** Criteria in EARS form with examples, the forced categories, the agreement check, the questions. Engineer: answer or accept defaults in the list view.
4. **S3.** The risk-map script runs on the brief's touched-area candidates, then the plan is written with its fixed tables (R-S3-18), each task carrying named validation recipes, and a script-generated readiness table on the first page (R-S3-21). The temporary semantic-contract checklist of R-S3-20 is part of this same artefact and decision, not another gate. Engineer: approve the criteria-and-plan bundle, or send back to S3, S2, or S1 with a send-back tag.
5. **S4.** The ticket worktree on the ticket branch, mounted read-write only inside the sandbox. One fresh invocation per plan-task attempt; the runner executes the task's named validation recipes once after the invocation. A failed attempt closes with its evidence and feeds the next fresh attempt; three failed attempts escalate. When the target branch has moved, a conflict-free advance whose upstream changes are disjoint from the plan's scope, the touched areas, the sensitive paths, and the diff, and that leaves the brief's impact evidence unchanged, is taken by the runner and followed by a validation-only rerun (R-S5-14); any other movement waits for the human refresh.
6. **S5.** A trusted preflight verifies the current plan approval, target-base, head, diff, deviation, sandbox, toolchain, and final reviewer-set identities and creates the review tuple. One R-I-14 sandbox provisions separate clean base and head copy-on-write views over immutable lower layers, with isolated disposable upper and cache layers, then runs in order: project lint, compile/type and unit tests; mandatory secret, static-analysis, dependency-vulnerability and licence-policy recipes; resolved-dependency verification; size; scope; source-declaration comparison; behavioural-contract evidence; and final approval-binding checks. Checks continue after a red result unless sandbox integrity or tuple construction fails. Failures and waivable blind spots return in one `red_check` item; Initial-scope, stale-evidence, ownership, trust, and sandbox failures are never waivable. A red result confined to the project's lint, compile/type, and unit-test recipes first returns to S4 for a bounded fix round before anyone is asked (R-S4-9). Engineer: only when an item exists.
7. **S6.** A trusted race guard first confirms the final reviewer set and approval binding are unchanged. It assembles the local packet with the diff appended and a separate PR-body narrative without the literal diff, both opening with the evidence table of R-S6-1. Each required reviewer approves or requests changes against one review subject and records an active-attention bucket. Full quorum creates `pr_create` for the first publication or `pr_update` for an approved revision; the outbox worker rechecks the subject, pushes only the ticket branch, and creates or updates the same **draft** pull request. The automated Initial path ends at `pr_opened`; the ticket remains open for R-H-11's manual outcome record while normal GitHub review and checks proceed outside Initial automation.

**Written to the record.** Every stage, child and utility run, tool call, guard decision, reviewer set, approval, waiver, evidence tuple, incident/control event, disposition, coverage attestation, and external-write intent as it happens, with exact runtime, adapter, model, sandbox, toolchain, trust-profile, manifest and recipe identities, tokens, cost provenance, and outcome. Every artefact is a versioned file with a table row; every tool result is such a file, a result under the inline limit is also returned inline, and a larger one reaches the agent only as a path and an excerpt (R-I-17). Every queue item, question, answer, assumption, deviation, generated-test identity, tag, index use, and manual pull-request outcome is recorded. The invocation envelope is reconstructable under R-I-15; the PRD never promises bit-identical hosted-model output. A text report over the views is available on demand and the governed ticket directory is exportable.

**Explicitly not there.** Automated S7: no polling GitHub, fetching Actions logs, detecting new commits, or detecting merge. The Initial human records the pull-request outcome; that is ledger capture, not S7 automation. Also absent: the advisory pass; observer scoring and calibration; the full quality-eval harness, benchmarks, and improvement agent beyond smoke/conformance fixtures and their directories; Confluence sync; dashboards; the read-only MCP server; stacked pull requests; sensitive-work admission; S1 fan-out; more than one ticket at a time.

**Definition of done.** Milestone A passes first. Then one eligible `small_feature` ticket on the pilot Java service goes from a Jira key to a draft pull request whose description is the approved narrative and whose native GitHub view carries the diff; every run, tool result used as evidence, boundary, human decision, and external write is recorded; no credential other than the scoped runtime key of R-I-14 enters an agent sandbox; no branch is pushed before full approval quorum; S5 and the PR-publication worker certify the same fresh review subject and referenced approved plan subject; and the eventual merge or abandonment is entered manually with the actual final SHA and body hash, approval and required-check dispositions, and production-coverage status. The report distinguishes first-attempt reliability, queue latency, active attention, and unavailable evidence. The ticket becomes the first redacted real fixture. Expansion happens only under R-O-13.
